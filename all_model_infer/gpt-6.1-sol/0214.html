Background and Scientific Goals

Our objective is to develop and mature wafer-scale thin-film
Magnesium Diboride (MgB2) enabling:

  • High performance superconducting nanowire single-photon
    detectors (SNSPDs) operating above 4K.
  • Superconducting filters and detectors for astrophysics and
    remote sensing in the sub-mm to far-IR regime.

This requires an accurate model of the both the equilibrium and
non-equilibrium superconductivity and quasiparticle dynamics in
a two-gap superconductor with various degrees of disorder and
interband interaction strength.

Outlook

  • Have developed a robust model of the 2-gap interaction in
    MgB2 applicable to films over a wide thickness, sheet
    resistance and Tc range.
  • Currently incorporating data from critical field measurements
    to constrain the total DOS.
  • Film-to-film comparisons can now inform fabrication and
    further work on modeling detector response.

Why MgB2?

Superconductivity in MgB2 is mediated by the electron-phonon
interaction. Uniquely among BCS superconductors, it exhibits:

  • 2 interacting superconducting band gaps: a strongly coupled 2D
    π band and a weakly coupled 3d σ band.
  • The resulting high superconducting transition temperature Tc
    offers a compelling pathway for superconducting devices that
    operate at elevated temperatures or at higher frequencies.

JPL has developed a process for wafer-scale MgB2 with low
roughness and high uniformity with Tc of up to 31 K for bulk films
and high kinetic inductance [1,2].

Approach and Results II: Modeling 2 gap physics

  • Obtain the temperature dependent gap energies Δπ(T) and
    Δσ(T) and the band interaction parameters Vππ, Vπσ, Vσσ.
  • To do so, we developed a self-consistent approach to fitting
    the measured penetration depth using Eliashberg Theory.
$\displaystyle\sum_{\beta}\left(V_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}-N_{\alpha}\cdot\Pi_{\alpha}\cdot\delta_{\alpha\beta}\right)\cdot\Delta_{\beta}=0$
$\displaystyle\Pi_{\alpha}=\int_0^{\omega_c}d\xi\,\frac{\tanh[\beta\sqrt{\xi^2+|\Delta_{\alpha}|^2}/2]}{\sqrt{\xi^2+|\Delta_{\alpha}|^2}}$
  • Using 3 fit parameters: ratio of the DOS, ratio of the gap
    energies at T=0, and fraction of conductivity from the π
    band, we accurately reproduce both the penetration depth
    and the dissipation as a function of temperature [3].
