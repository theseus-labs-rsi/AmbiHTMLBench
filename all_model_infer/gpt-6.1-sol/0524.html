THE WEEKLY REVIEW

Mr. McCully Wins Out in
Competition

Our splendid subject “Raising the First American Flag” used in conjunction with a good distribution plan, that of distributing calendars through schools, as suggested on page No. 124 of hand book, landed this order, which amounts to $113.00. The plan con-
templates the advertiser sending a large sized mount 16x21 into the school rooms of the county, and the small sized 7x10 mounts are given by the teachers to pupils, and thus go into the homes. By referring to the hand book you will find that pupils are supposed to write essays on the subject. We believe this distribution plan a very good one. This same buyer also uses a lot of our subject “In the Heart of the Rockies” for entirely different distribution. Mr. McCully’s letter which ac-
companied the order follows:

The enclosed order was taken in active competition with the Murphy Co. Mr. Buell, their representative in this territory showing his line at the same time. I won out easily, the large calendars go to the school rooms in the country and the teacher furnishes the youngsters with small ones. These people have bought of Murphy for several years, Mr. Buell liv-
ing but a short distance from here. As far as I can learn Murphy got one order from a wholesale liquor man here. I will get at least $200.00 more here. Had to stay over here in order to show my line against Murphy’s.

Yours very truly,E. W. McCully.

A Few Arguments Mr. Cies
Uses in Closing Early
Orders

Mr. Cies is going along at a merry clip and he says he has not found much time to write. In sending in a report for the 6th, amounting to $187.30, the following letter was attached:

I have been too busy to write you sooner and tonight I have nothing special to offer. We have such an excellent line and I have had so many experiences with it that I don’t know which ones to tell, and I can’t tell all.

True I have been sending in some small orders every day, but they have not come easy, several of them it has taken from

three to six hours to close, but I never let go of a man as long as he keeps his inter-
est, and I don’t stay long with a fellow that won’t enthuse over it with me.

I was from 8:30 till 12:00 closing my mill order today, they had decided not to buy calendars, had distributed but few of the ones they bought. I outlined a plan of distributing them that won and finally got me their order for the beautiful “Autumn” De Luxe for 1910.

Talking to a man yesterday he said, “It seems to me your calendars are awfully high,” I said “they are, they are higher than a cat’s back, but they are worth every cent we are asking for them, and you will admit it if you compare them with the junk you bought last year.” He bought.

Another man said “Something cheap will do just as well.” I said “alright, suppose you were going to visit all your customers personally, would you fix up and look your best, or would you just slouch out any old way?” He said “I would fix up of course,” then I said, “In-
asmuch as you cannot call upon all of your customers personally, don’t you think your greeting ought to be worthy of the man it represents, and as it is a souvenir, do you want it to have the appearance of something real cheap, or the best of its kind that money can buy?” He bought.

A banker said to me, “I’ll take the num-
bers of two or three” and before he said any more, I said “That won’t buy you any-
thing,” he said “Why not?” “Because” I replied “they will still be in the line, and I will sell everyone of my best subjects before I get back to you, and you’ll have to take fourth or fifth choice,” he said, “O well I’ll buy of someone else there are plenty of others,” “O yes” I answered, “but suppose you treat them all like you are treating me, don’t you see you will possibly be up against the same proposi-
tion.” He said “O well I wouldn’t treat them all that way,” and then I asked him what I had done to him that he should make an example of me. He bought.
