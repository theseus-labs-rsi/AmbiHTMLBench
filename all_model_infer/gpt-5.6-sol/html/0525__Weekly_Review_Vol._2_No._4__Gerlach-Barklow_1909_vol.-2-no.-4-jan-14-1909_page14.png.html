Mr. Reilly Tells of a Cal-
endar Display in Albany

Mr. Reilly’s letter follows:

An incident in the calendar business has
taken place in this city, during the past
week. The Grand Union Tea Co. has
filled their show window with all the cal-
endars of the different business interests
in town, and the Albany Hardware & Iron
Company have the same display also.
Needless to say that I spent an hour with
my note book and pencil, as a boost for
the calendar game it is great. I was glad
to see many G.-B. subjects add distinction
to the collection.
J. G. Reilly.

In another letter, addressed to the Editor,
Mr. Reilly speaks of the Review as an inspira-
tion to him, and we hope he will make it pos-
sible for us to tell about some of his stunts be-
fore long. His letter follows:

Have today received the 3rd copy of the
Review and the special. I like to read
about the performances of the stars, and
although I cannot hope this year to be a
star they inspire me to keep in the pro-
cession.
J. G. Reilly.

Mr. Whittier Congratulates Mr.
K. H. Gerlach on His
First Day’s Business

In writing us Jan. 3rd Mr. Whittier writes

I am glad to note by the weekly Review
that some of the boys are getting early

A Compliment to the G.-B. Line

Mr. A. E. Gerlach last year sold a small bill
of calendars to a prominent banker and broker
in Chicago, who is considered an art connois-
seur and who owns a large private collection,

many of which are in the Art Institute. This
year he called upon this gentleman early and
succeeded in selling him a bill amounting close
onto $150.00. He buys these calendars more
as a souvenir than as an advertisement, and
gives them away to his personal friends and his
customers. It is therefore not surprising that
he selected several different subjects. Mr.
Gerlach writes as follows:

Mr.—— is a prominent banker and
broker of Chicago and an art connois-
seur of renown, having a collection of
originals in the art institute here and a
private collection at his home. He had
never purchased calendars until May, and
he already turned down several men, but
I simply asked him to look at the G.-B.
line, I wanted his artistic opinion. I got
same, also an order for 700 mounts. On
his desk was a circular “wait for the Os-
borne Man,” and he had just opened B.
& B.’s house calendar, but he could not
wait. He said our line was beyond com-
pare, and predicts success to all G.-B.
salesmen.
A. E. Gerlach.

In another letter from Mr. A. E. Gerlach he
writes as follows:

My sales for week 12-28-08 to 1-2-09
amount to $674.22. Experts on art and
high grade calendars here say our line
gives the impression that we have been in
business 20 years instead of one. Every-
body speaks of the vast improvement of
the 1910 line over the 1909 line.
Yours truly,
A. E. Gerlach.

We have some splendid letters from Mr.
Cies, Mr. Finley, Mr. White, and several
others, which we will not be able to use this
week, but we will have them ready for you next
week. They are the kind of letters that en-
thuse. They tell of victories won strictly on
the merits of the goods. Of course, to this
should be added a fair share of credit for good
salesmanship for, after all, the best kind of
calendars do not sell themselves except in rare
instances.
