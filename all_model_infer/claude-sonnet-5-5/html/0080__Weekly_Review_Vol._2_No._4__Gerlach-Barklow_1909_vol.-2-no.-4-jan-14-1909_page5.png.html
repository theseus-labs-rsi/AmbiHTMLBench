The Week in Detail

We have already said so much about the first week’s business that there remains but little to add.

As you know, Mr. T. R. Gerlach leads the bunch, with 26 orders, amounting to $3233.61. Oddly enough, after he got away one of his customers sent in an additional order amounting to over $100.00. They got up a special calendar and didn’t receive the goods until the day after Mr. Gerlach arrived. They made such a tremendous hit that they ordered an additional 750.

Mr. K. H. Gerlach secured 12 orders, amounting to $2169.00, which makes him rank No. 2. Mention has already been made of his orders.

Mr. Finley, with three orders, is No. 3, with over $1400.00. He got his business by doing considerable tall jumping. Although his territory is a compact one, he made a jump of 150 miles. On the morning of December 28th, at 8:30 o’clock, he was at the office of one of his good old customers who had bought of him when he was selling for the Osborne Co. Early as he was, the Osborne man had been there ahead of him, but didn’t get an audience. He was told to come next day. The buyer had been drilled by the advertising of the Osborne

Co. and by their extravagant claims to think that there was nothing to it but the Osborne line, so would not place his order until he had an opportunity to see it. Furthermore, they didn’t intend to buy until March, anyhow. Then he made his jump of 150 miles, and we are going to let him tell about it in his own way. We have not the space for his letter this week. It is enough to say that it was another case of assuming that the Osborne Co. were the only ones who could furnish calendars really worth while. It took him a good share of the day, but he got an order for $454.00 and to quote from his letter: “The President and Cashier both said it was the finest line they ever saw.” He then made another jump to see a Brewery. He found the proprietor sick and the force exceedingly busy, but he got their order for $394.00. Mr. Finley called on several other buyers during the week, but was unable to actually close the business, and on Saturday he returned to his first prospect, according to previous engagement, and found both the Osborne and Murphy salesmen on hand. He won out again easily, with an order for $581.00, the buyer remarking that the Osborne line was the poorest of the three.

We cannot resist quoting from Mr. Finley’s letter this paragraph:

“I wish to add that it seems the hardest thing for old Osborne customers to realize that it is possible for a new Company to break in with goods near their standing, and were it in our power to get them to wait to see our line before they buy, there would be but one finish to the famous Osborne Co.”

Mr. Coleman, with 14 orders, has $1061.00 to his credit. He did no startling stunts, but had a good, steady business for five days of the week. We were in error last week when we said he got his business in Canada. He did a little work in the United States just across the border the first week before tackling the Canadians.

Mr. Cies is No. 5, with 14 orders, amounting to over $800.00. His business is all of the ordinary kind, in fact, he began with only $18.00 on Monday and wound up with $81.00 on Saturday, but his business ranged from $147.00 to $247.00 per day during the balance of the week.

Mr. Ungerer duplicated Mr. Cies’ performance almost, his total being $14.00 less and the number of orders being exactly the same. His best day was Monday, when he reported four orders amounting to over $400.00.
