Author: Albert Wandui, JPL Postdoctoral Fellow (365H)

Background and Scientific Goals

Our objective is to develop and mature wafer-scale thin-film Magnesium Diboride (MgB₂) enabling:

  • High performance superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors (SNSPDs) operating above 4K.
  • Superconducting filters and detectors for astrophysics and remote sensing in the sub-mm to far-IR regime.

This requires an accurate model of the both the equilibrium and non-equilibrium superconductivity and quasiparticle dynamics in a two-gap superconductor with various degrees of disorder and interband interaction strength.

Outlook

  • Have developed a robust model of the 2-gap interaction in MgB₂ applicable to films over a wide thickness, sheet resistance and T_c range.
  • Currently incorporating data from critical field measurements to constrain the total DOS.
  • Film-to-film comparisons can now inform fabrication and further work on modeling detector response.

Why MgB₂?

Superconductivity in MgB₂ is mediated by the electron-phonon interaction. Uniquely among BCS superconductors, it exhibits:

  • 2 interacting superconducting band gaps: a strongly coupled 2D π band and a weakly coupled 3d σ band.
  • The resulting high superconducting transition temperature T_c offers a compelling pathway for superconducting devices that operate at elevated temperatures or at higher frequencies.

JPL has developed a process for wafer-scale MgB₂ with low roughness and high uniformity with T_c of up to 31 K for bulk films and high kinetic inductance [1,2].

Approach and Results II: Modeling 2 gap physics

  • Obtain the temperature dependent gap energies Δ_π(T) and Δ_σ(T) and the band interaction parameters V^ππ, V^πσ, V^σσ.
  • To do so, we developed a self-consistent approach to fitting the measured penetration depth using Eliashberg Theory.
$$\sum_{\beta} \left(V_{\alpha\beta}^{-1} - N_{\alpha} \cdot \Pi_{\alpha} \cdot \delta_{\alpha\beta}\right) \cdot \Delta_{\beta} = 0$$
$$\Pi_{\alpha} = \int_0^{\omega_c} d\xi \frac{\tanh[\beta\sqrt{\xi^2 + |\Delta_{\alpha}|^2}/2]}{\sqrt{\xi^2 + |\Delta_{\alpha}|^2}}$$
  • Using 3 fit parameters: ratio of the DOS, ratio of the gap energies at T=0, and fraction of conductivity from the π band, we accurately reproduce both the penetration depth and the dissipation as a function of temperature [3].
