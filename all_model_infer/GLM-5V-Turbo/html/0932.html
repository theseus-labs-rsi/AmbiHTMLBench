Like any creative work, walking for design purposes has to be practiced. Walking designers want to focus on five things. First, learn to open up perception by avoiding comment and evaluation. Second, learn to switch between avoiding comment and reflection. Third, work on letting go and enjoying the creative process of reorientation. And fifth, release traumatisms first idea into designs. Therefore, walking for designers should mean to assume the role of a trainee (Schneekloth, 1987:93).

To ensure that all acquired knowledge and ideas can be operative for design processes, the experienced characteristics, atmospheres, and emotions need to be documented. This can happen through writing, sketching, or documenting that is a way to translate experiences. Experimenting with walking

Back in our group of landscape architects, urban planners and geographers in the periphery of Hamburg. On 6 April 2013, they walked some 20 km, starting from the town centre of Lüneburg towards the nature reserve (Landschaftsschutzgebiet) and mead again in Königsmoor (Landschafs Saale).

On their search for ideas for the landscapes of the Harburger Berge, the Atlas Land, and parts of the river Elbe, they applied a set of rules. They did not talk while they were walking but rather tried to navigate the landscape by following their intuition and using their compasses. They walked most of the journey alone but tried to talk to people they met on their way, in order to learn more about the landscapes. The walkers met every hour or so to share experiences and thoughts. In this way, the other rule was to find their own individual rhythm, and a fourth was they immersed themselves into the landscapes, trying so enjoy the experience and what they termed 'play walking'. Before and after the walks, they documented their images and questions that resulted from the walks. They also made sound recordings of the environment, experienced the traversed space and enjoyed being part of the Geestdow with animals, other walkers, the changing weather, and the like.
