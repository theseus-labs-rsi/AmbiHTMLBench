Mr. Reilly Tells of a Calendar Display in Albany

Mr. Reilly's letter follows:

An incident in the calendar business has taken place in this city, during the past week. The Grand Union Tea Co. has filled their show window with all the calendars of the different business interests in town, and the Albany Hardware & Iron Company have the same display also. Needless to say that I spent an hour with my note book and pencil, as a boost for the calendar game it is great. I was glad to see many G.-B. subjects add distinction to the collection.

J. G. REILLY.

In another letter, addressed to the Editor, Mr. Reilly speaks of the Review as an inspiration to him, and we hope he will make it possible for us to tell about some of his stunts before long. His letter follows:

Have today received the 3rd copy of the Review and the special. I like to read about the performances of the stars, and although I cannot hope this year to be a star they inspire me to keep in the pro-cession.

J. G. REILLY.

A Compliment to the G.-B. Line

Mr. A. E. Gerlach last year sold a small bill of calendars to a prominent banker and broker in Chicago, who is considered an art connoisseur and who owns a large private collection,

many of which are in the Art Institute. This year he called upon this gentleman early and succeeded in selling him a bill amounting close onto $150.00. He buys these calendars more as a souvenir than as an advertisement, and gives them away to his personal friends and his customers. It is therefore not surprising that he selected several different subjects. Mr. Gerlach writes as follows:

Mr.——— is a prominent banker and broker of Chicago and an art connoisseur of renown, having a collection of originals in the art institute here and a private collection at his home. He had never purchased calendars until May, and he already turned down several men, but I simply asked him to look at the G.-B. line, I wanted his artistic opinion. I got same, also an order for 700 mounts. On his desk was a circular "wait for the Osborne Man," and he had just opened B. &B.'s house calendar, but he could not wait. He said our line was beyond compare, and predicts success to all G.-B. salesmen.

A. E. GERLACH.

In another letter from Mr. A. E. Gerlach he writes as follows:

My sales for week 12-28-08 to 1-2-09 amount to $674.92. Experts on art and high grade calendars here say our line gives the impression that we have been in business 20 years instead of one. Everybody speaks of the vast improvement of the 1910 line over the 1909 line.

Yours truly,

A. E. GERLACH.
