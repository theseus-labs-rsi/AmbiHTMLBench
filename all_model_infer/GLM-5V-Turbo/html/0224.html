Exploring the Effect of Temperature
on Enzyme Activity in Plant Cells

Alex G. Monica V. Tessa H. Aiko S. Bill T.
Introduction
Enzymes are essential biological catalysts that regulate chemical reactions within cells. This study investigates how temperature influences enzyme activity in plant cells, specifically focusing on amylase, an enzyme that catalyzes the breakdown of starch. Understanding the relationship between temperature and enzyme activity can help optimize agricultural practices, such as enhancing crop resistance to temperature fluctuations and improving the efficiency of industrial processes that involve plant-based enzymes.
Methodology
Sample Preparation
Corn leaf cells were isolated and homogenized to extract amylase enzymes. The enzyme activity was measured by the rate of starch breakdown.
Temperature Variation
Enzyme activity was tested at $0^\circ\text{C}$, $20^\circ\text{C}$, $40^\circ\text{C}$, $60^\circ\text{C}$, and $80^\circ\text{C}$. Reactions were incubated for 30 minutes at each temperature.
Substrate Addition
Starch solution was added to each reaction, providing a substrate for the amylase enzyme.
Reaction Termination
The reactions were stopped after 30 minutes using an enzyme inhibitor, and glucose production was measured.
Data Analysis
Absorbance of glucose was measured using a spectrophotometer. Data were analyzed using ANOVA to assess statistical significance of temperature on enzyme activity.
Results
Optimal Temperature
Enzyme activity peaked at $40^\circ\text{C}$, where the amylase was most effective in catalyzing starch breakdown. At this temperature, the rate of glucose production was 50% higher than at lower or higher temperatures.
Temperature Effect
  • At $0^\circ\text{C}$, enzyme activity was minimal, with only 10% of the maximum activity observed at $40^\circ\text{C}$
  • At $20^\circ\text{C}$, enzyme activity was moderate, with a 30% increase in glucose production compared to $0^\circ\text{C}$
  • At $60^\circ\text{C}$, enzyme activity dropped significantly, with only 25% of the activity seen at $40^\circ\text{C}$
  • At $80^\circ\text{C}$, enzyme activity was nearly zero, indicating enzyme denaturation

Discussion
The results suggest that temperature has a profound impact on enzyme activity, with amylase being most efficient at $40^\circ\text{C}$. The significant decline in activity at temperatures above $60^\circ\text{C}$ supports the hypothesis that extreme heat denatures enzymes. This has implications for both agricultural biotechnology, where temperature resilience could be engineered, and for industrial enzyme applications, where optimal reaction conditions are critical for efficiency.
Conclusion
Amylase activity in plant cells is highly temperature-dependent, with an optimal temperature of $40^\circ\text{C}$ for maximum efficiency. Understanding the temperature sensitivity of enzymes can help improve crop productivity under varying environmental conditions and optimize industrial enzyme processes.
