Exclusive: top Tory MP defects to Labour in fury at NHS crisis
● Ex-health minister slams 'nationalist right'
● 'Tories failing on improving care'
Toby Helm
Political Editor

A Tory MP and former health minister has staged a dramatic defection to Labour after concluding that the Conservatives have become a "nationalist party of the right" that has abandoned compassion and no longer prioritises improving the NHS.

In an exclusive interview with the Observer, Dan Poulter, who has been Conservative MP for Central Suffolk and North Ipswich, where he also works as an NHS hospital doctor, announced in an exclusive interview with the Observer that he was resigning as a Tory MP and would join Labour immediately until the next election.

He said he would not seek re-election as a Labour candidate but wanted to use the next general election, but writing in today's Observer, he says he regrets a role advising the Labour party on its health policies while believing there is more hope of the NHS work being turned around under Keir Starmer.

Poulter said his experiences on the front line over 20 nights shifts over the last year had convinced him that the department and been "truly life-changing" and persuaded him that the Conservative Party he believed was now really committed to investing in improving the NHS.

He said: "I could not go on as part of that I have to be able to look my NHS colleagues in the eye, my patients in the eye, and say I am doing everything I can to help you. And I know that the Conservative government has been failing on the NHS and its patients", which is the reason for my decision."

The Observer understands that discussions between Poulter and senior Labour figures have been going on for many months at the highest level of the party. Poulter's decision is likely to play a major role in Labour's general election campaign, with the benefit of his firsthand inside knowledge.
