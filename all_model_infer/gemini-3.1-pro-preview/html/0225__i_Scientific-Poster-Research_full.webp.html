Introduction

Superconductivity is a state of zero electrical resistance occurring at low temperatures in certain materials. High-temperature superconductors (HTS), such as yttrium barium copper oxide (YBCO), exhibit superconductivity above the boiling point of liquid nitrogen. This study explores how varying magnetic fields affect the superconducting properties of YBCO ceramics, providing insight into their practical applications in energy transmission and magnetic levitation.

Methodology

We synthesized YBCO ceramic samples with varying compositions of yttrium (Y), barium (Ba), copper (Cu), and oxygen (O). Using a cryostat, we cooled the samples to temperatures ranging from 77 K to 90 K. Magnetic fields ranging from 0 to 5 Tesla were applied perpendicularly and parallel to the sample surface. The critical temperature was determined by measuring the resistivity of the samples as a function of temperature in the presence of the magnetic field. The data were analyzed to find the values of Tc and Hc2

Discussion

Our results demonstrate that magnetic fields significantly alter the superconducting properties of YBCO ceramics, with field orientation and strength playing critical roles in Tc and Hc2. The anisotropic behavior observed in the samples suggests that further research is needed to optimize YBCO materials for practical applications in magnetic environments, such as in high-speed magnetic levitation trains and power transmission lines.

Conclusion

Magnetic field-induced suppression of superconductivity in high-temperature ceramic superconductors can be mitigated by adjusting the composition and the orientation of the magnetic field relative to the sample. Future studies should focus on enhancing flux pinning mechanisms to extend the functional temperature range of YBCO superconductors in magnetic fields.
