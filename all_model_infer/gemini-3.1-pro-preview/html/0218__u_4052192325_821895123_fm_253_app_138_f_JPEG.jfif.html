to Labour in fury at NHS crisis

  • Ex-health minister slams 'nationalist right'

Toby Helm Political Editor

A Tory MP and former health minis-ter has staged a dramatic defection to Labour, saying the Conservatives have become a "nationalist party of the right" that has abandoned compassion and no longer priori-tises the NHS.

Dr Dan Poulter, the MP for Central Suffolk and North Ipswich, who works part-time as a mental health doctor in an NHS hospital, announced in an exclusive interview with the Observer that he was resigning as a Tory MP and would be taking the Labour whip until the next election.

He said he would not seek re-elec-tion to the House of Commons at the next general election. But, writing in today's Observer, he says he envisages a role advising the Labour party on its policies on mental health while focus-ing more on his NHS work.

Poulter said his experiences on more than 20 night shifts over the last year in a severely overstretched acci-dent and emergency department had been "truly life-changing" and per-suaded him to defect to the only party he believed was now really committed to investing in improving the NHS.

The defection has, however, been kept the tightest of secrets, with only half a dozen people in the party knowing it was coming before the Observer broke the news.

A commercial blood product at the centre of the biggest treatment scan-dal in the history of the NHS was approved for use after government officials were told convicts were among the paid donors and virus contamination "should be assumed", corporate filings reveal.

The product, given to haemophiliacs in the 1970s and '80s to enable their blood to clot, was injected into thou-sands of UK patients, including young children, who were then infected with HIV and hepatitis C.

A single batch could contain plasma from 20,000 donors har-vested in US prisons and some of the poorest neighbourhoods of the US, where drug use and sexual infec-tions were rife. One infected donation would contaminate the entire batch.

An inquiry lead by Brian Langstaff will report next month on the scandal, which has killed about 3,000 people in the UK. He has already concluded "wrongs were done at individual, col-lective and systemic levels".
