The National Centre
for Neurology and Brain
Mechanics Presents

Cognitive
Dissonance:
Performing
Menial Tasks

Study conducted by Jonas Festinger,
Marcus Carlsmith & Gabriel Chessman

[Illustration: three head silhouettes containing a gear, an exclamation mark, and a question mark]

REFERENCES:
Walsh, Karen., Kemputo, Odelle., “The psychology of self-deception”
The Atlantic Journal, 2017 • Satuda, Evelyn., Timoteo, Anya., “Neuroscience
of Decision-Making” The Science Review, 2018 • Brahms, Hansen., Leigh,
Anderson., “The Need and Neurology for Happiness”, The Arch Journal, 2020

Purpose:

Festinger, Carlsmith and
Chessman wanted to
understand if making
people perform a menial
task would result in
cognitive dissonance as
a result of forced
compliance behavior.

[Illustration: gears above a group of people]

Hypothesis:

When asked to do an
unenjoyable task, people
will not readily admit as
much, and tell themselves
and others it is actually
enjoyable.

[Illustration: thumbs-up above balance scales]

Participants:

In a lab setting, 71 male
students as participants
to perform a series of
menial tasks (like turning
pegs in a peg board for
an hour).

[Illustration: clock above a peg board]

Method:

The participants were
then paid either $1 or $20
to tell a waiting participant
(a test confederate) that
the tasks were really
engaging and fun. Almost
all of the participants
agreed to walk into the
waiting room and
persuade the confederate
that the boring experiment
would be interesting.

[Illustration: speech bubble above a hand receiving money]

Results:

When the participants
were asked to evaluate
the experiment, the
participants who were
paid only $1 rated the
tedious task as more
fun and enjoyable than
the participants who
were paid $20 to lie.

[Illustration: upward arrow above a star containing a plus sign]

Conclusion:

Getting paid $1 is not
sufficient for lying, so
those who were paid $1
experienced dissonance.
These participants could
only overcome that
dissonance by coming
to believe that the tasks
really were interesting
and enjoyable.

Being paid $20 provides
a reason, therefore there
is no dissonance.
