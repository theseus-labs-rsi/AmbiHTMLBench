Postdoc Research

Determination of the Interior Structure of Europa
from Gravity and Magnetic Data

Flavio Petricca (JPL Postdoctoral Fellow, 322)
J. Castillo-Rogez (4000), S. Vance (322), M. Melwani Daswani (322), C. Cochrane (322)

Background

The Galileo mission provided measurements of Europa's gravity and magnetic fields, indicating that the moon hosts a subsurface ocean and that its interior is mostly made by rocks (90 wt. %). However, the interior structure is still poorly understood, with the main uncertainties affecting the ice shell thickness and ocean depth, and the size of a putative metallic core whose existence was inferred from the first analysis of Galileo’s gravity data (Anderson et al., 1998). A recent reanalysis indicated a smaller level of differentiation (Gomez-Casajus et al., 2021), challenging the inference of the existence of the metallic core. Knowledge of the degree of differentiation is crucial to understand the origins and evolution of Europa. Furthermore, gravity and magnetic data have only been used separately, and developing methodologies to jointly analyze them will be fundamental for Europa Clipper’s investigations.

Objectives

We analyze measurements of Europa’s gravity and magnetic fields based on Galileo data with new generation interior models and techniques to answer the following questions:

  1. Did Europa differentiate a metallic core? What is the composition of the rocky mantle?
  2. How can we combine gravity and magnetic data in an effective way to infer the ice thickness and ocean depth and salinity?

Approach and Results

We developed new interior models that use Bayesian inference and Markov-chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithms to combine gravity and magnetic observations with geochemical data on the composition of the ocean and the rocky mantle. Our two main results are:

  1. Our models indicate that Europa did not differentiate a metallic core (Figure 1). This implies that Europa’s evolution was colder than previously thought. The resulting structure and composition of the rocky mantle indicates that Europa and the Jupiter system likely formed from dry CV chondrite-like material.
  2. The combination of gravity and magnetic data can separately constrain the ice shell and ocean thicknesses, while separate inversions are degenerate (Figure 2).

Significance of Results/Benefits to NASA/JPL

The unprecedented constraint on the structure of the deep interior changes our understanding of the evolution of Europa. The lack of a metallic core indicates a cold evolution, with implications for the origin of the Jupiter system and the evolution of the other Galilean moons. The methodology developed to combine gravity and magnetic data will be fundamental in the context of Europa Clipper’s interior science. This technique will provide a framework to connect interior models with the results of ongoing lab work at JPL (PSIE, PI: S. Vance). New experimental data on the thermodynamic and electric properties of solutions relevant to Europa’s ocean are being collected and will be implemented in this interior modeling framework.

Future Work

The framework developed will be expanded to include new experimental data in preparation for the analysis of Europa Clipper’s data. It will be also applied to Ganymede, Callisto and Titan to explore their interiors. Finally, ongoing work includes the application of this framework to the moons of Uranus in the context of the formulation of the Uranus Orbiter and Probe, highly prioritized by the Planetary Science Decadal Survey.
