. . . . . . WORKSHOP REVIEW . . . . .

Around fifty people gathered at the Cathedral of St. Catharine of Sienna, Pa., on Saturday February 25th for a Sacred Dance Workshop, "The Liturgy of Worship in Dance.   Our guest leader was Mary Jane Wolbers assisted by Geraldine Stofco.  The workshop was coordinated by members of the Sacred Dance Guild in cooperation with St. Catharine of Siena.

The morning began with a colorful getting-to-know you improvisation followed by a warm-up technique class.  This was led by Geraldine Stofco.  After our bag lunches we saw the film "The Liturgy of Worship in Dance".  The theme of the afternoon was choreographic development: setting a mood for welcome, prayer, meditation, communion, so you want to dance a hymn?  Mnnnn!; a dance for a special occasion; to carol is to dance. These sessions were directed by Mary Jane Wolbers.  Each participant chose an area to explore.  At the end of the day each group shared what their group had developed through movement.  The day was an educational learning process.  The participants' enthusiasm and willingness to learn and share made the day a heartwarming, creative experience.

LATE LATE LATE NEWS

From Gina Ball:  Have just finished my my master's thesis production at California State University, Northridge. I choreographed a 40 minute work with original music by Theodore Peterson. Titled "Dark Illuminations" it was a multi-media work dealing with the place of man in the universe.

From Rusty MacLeod, Mt. Pleasant, Michigan:  I had a wonderful experience recently which I want to share with my SDG friends.  Was asked to speak at a "Feminar" at one of our local churches, and to dance.  The theme of the day was "Be Thou My Vision," and I was to speak on the subject of praise in prayer.  Since I had just finished

memorizing the 103rd Psalm, I assumed that I'd be dancing either to that or to the hymn-theme.  I asked for the Lord's help and set to work.  The movement that came to me was so lovely, I knew it had to come from Him.  However, when I tried to set it to either the hymn or the Psalm, it seemed all wrong, and I just couldn't understand! After some time, I suddenly remembered that the leader had intended to close the day with "Spirit of the Living God, Fall Afresh On Me."  And sure enough that was exactly what the movement was suited for!  The day went so beautifully, I used it again twice in my own fellowship, and everyone said it was most edifying.  Praise God!

I taught the group the little hymn, "Spirit of the Living God," and they accompanied me with their voices.  Note:  If you intend to use this type of prayer accompaniment, be sure to warn the people to keep their eyes open.  They may not realize you intend to dance to it, and keep their eyes closed!

Very soon I will be  beginning work on my master's thesis for Central Michigan University, entitled "Dancing for Jesus."...Tentative plans are to do a first volume on the history of dance in the Christian church, and then follow it up with a specialist-degree work, perhaps with the same title, Vol. II, as a current-status work including practical application.

From Marjorie Coco, Louisville, Kentucky: Three girls did symbolic candle movements Christmas Eve to congregational singing of "Silent Night".  A few ideas we used were: "See the Eastern Wise Men Bring Gifts and Homage to Our King" - slow bend toward center of lying baby.  "Angels Sing Alleluias to Our King" - dancers turn outward toward congregation in "joy" position (legs apart, arms high and apart, head high.  "Star" - dancers in circle facing each other, raising candles high above head slowly almost touching one another.  Our adult dance choir is very young and we use choir gowns.  They are in the process of designing dresses  at this time.
