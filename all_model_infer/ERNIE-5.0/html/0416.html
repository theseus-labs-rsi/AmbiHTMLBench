But then we began to worry. We thought about how people with the fixed mindset focus too much on their ability: "Is it high enough?" "Will it look good enough?" "Will it make my deep, underlying ability look good enough?" But wouldn't praising people's ability focus them on it even more and, even worse, wouldn't it make them think that's what we value? Isn't that teaching them the fixed mindset? Isn't it telling them that all we care about is their performance from their underlying ability?

Adam Guettel has been called the crown prince and savior of musical theater. He's the grandson of Richard Rodgers, the man who wrote the music to such classics as Oklahoma! and Carousel. Guettel's mother gushes about her son's genius. So does everyone else. "The talent is there and it's major," raved a review in The New York Times. The question is: Does this kind of praise encourage people?

What do you think? I don't really know. However, I think we can ask these kinds of questions in our studies and see what happens.
