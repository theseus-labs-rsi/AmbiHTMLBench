Gerotranscendent Generativity:  How Older Adults Maintain a Focus on “Being” for Others

Theresa Southam, MA, PhD Student; Fielding Graduate University

ABSTRACT

Generativity in older adults has predominantly been defined as tangible assets delivered to others, including caring for others, civic action, and community volunteerism (Hodgkin, 2012; De St Aubin & McAdams, 2004). Less light has been shone on non-physical contributions. In this research we learn how generative older adults focus on being rather than doing, while becoming what Edmondson (2015) calls socio-cultural message bearers, advisors on the human condition and creators of cultural and artistic products.

RESEARCH GOALS, PARTICIPANTS AND METHODS

This ethnographic and phenomenologically informed research asked “What does it look like when older adults are contributing to the next generations from a gerotranscendent lens?”

Over two months, the researcher observed:

  • 14 participants in Intergenerational Leadership and Learning class co-facilitated by researcher.
  • Eight older adults recommended by a hospice society interlocutor who participated in two, 3-hour sessions: autobiographical life review (Wengraf, 2004) and life-cross roads (Keisari & Palgi, 2017).
  • Three participants randomly selected from whole sample for phenomenological interviews.
FIELDING
Graduate University

RESULTS

Records of group discussions, artwork, life crossroads diagrams, a survey and observations were coded as typifications – groupings based on shared experiences. A theme, raised independently by the participants during the group discussions and vigourously agreed on by all, is highlighted.

Typifications

Elders/Sages/Mentors: focus on just being by talking to people that no one will talk to, acting in a way that teaches and shows, communing with nature, “adopting” children that need soul grandparents, peer counseling and bringing spirit into the body.

Third Agers: focus on doing by being remembered for actions such as reinvigorating a community organization, advocating for more environmentally sound transportation, volunteering, and tutoring.

Overarching Theme

A focus on being rather than doing

“I feel sorry for people trying to save the world, I have a quieter more contemplative life to look forward to at 55.”

“The older I get, people look to me, I have so much gratitude.”

CONCLUSIONS AND NEXT STEPS

Gerotranscendence described as where the boundaries between self and the universe dissolve.

Study participants agree on a shift in emphasis from age 55 where they were doing to 70 when they are being.

“People measure how much we give materialistically. They know there’s more and they feel compelled to share love.” Quote from research participant

Next Steps

Although older adults are largely defined in contemporary EuroAmerican culture as a burden, they are in fact abundantly generative. The ability of older adults to continue learning, leading spiritual, artistic and contemplative lives, is supported by neurological studies (Cohen, 2005). Further research on the impact on the collective consciousness of adults’ messages, advice and creations is warranted.
