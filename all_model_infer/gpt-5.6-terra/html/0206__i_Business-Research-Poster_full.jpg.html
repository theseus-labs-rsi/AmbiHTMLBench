The sales effect of “Free App of the Day”
on Amazon Appstore: An empirical study

Hong Chen, Kevin Lachaud, Wenqi Zhou

INTRODUCTION

Apps, as an abbreviation of mobile applications, are software products for mobile platforms (Tang, 2016). They are designed to perform certain tasks on mobile devices (Islam, Islam, & Mazumder, 2010). This product category has grown to become a gigantic differentiated market with over 3.48 million apps on Google Play, an app marketplace, and the recent projection of over 935-billion-dollar revenue in 2023 (Statista Research, 2021a, Statista Research, 2021b).

OBJECTIVE

This work studies the adverse effect of “Free App of the Day” at Amazon Appstore.

[Image: hand using smartphone]

METHODOLOGY

  1. Literature review
    1. Digital free sampling
    2. eWOM
  2. Hypotheses
    1. H1 - Online user decisions to adopt apps are discouraged by the price rebound after the zero-price offering of FAD ends. The sharper price rebound leads to a greater decrease in app adoptions.
    2. H2 - Conditional on the total volume of eWOM, organic reviews are more influential to online user decisions of adopting apps than promotion-induced reviews after the FAD promotion ends.
  3. Data
    1. Context and data collection
    2. Key variables
  4. Empirical analysis
    1. Exploratory analysis
    2. Regression model and results

RESULTS

We notice that a few apps were off the direct spotlight post-promotion; however, they still offer zero price for another one or two days.
Specifically, an increase in SkewDailyi indicates a more right skewed distribution of daily number of reviews during the post-promotion period.
