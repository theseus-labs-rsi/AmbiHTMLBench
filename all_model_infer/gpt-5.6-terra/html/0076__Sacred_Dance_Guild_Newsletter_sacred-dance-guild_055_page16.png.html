News from Canada, Margo Evans: Dancers, clergy and church program directors met with the board of dir- ectors of CHOROS: A Project in Lit- urgical Dance, on March 2, 1978 to discuss what they were doing in the field of sacred dance. Some of our goals in meeting included understand- ing what attracted those present to this special ministry and form of prayer expression, considering ways for the church to encourage opportun- ities for dance and movement in lit- urgy and exploring what CHOROS might do to advance educational and experi- mental opportunities for greater use of dance and movement in liturgy.
CHOROS is a group of people from various church backgrounds in Toronto who are interested in seeing that greater attention is given to movement and dance in Christian worship. The ggroup has been meeting for about one and a half years. Beginning in an ad hoc way, with membership drawn mostly from the United Church of Canada, and without any specific mandate, it has gradually developed its membership and its mission. It is currently seeking to be incorporated as an ecumenical enterprise around the following purpose:
a. To encourage and support the use of movement and dance in liturgical celebrations.
b. To foster educational opportunities for children, youth and adults, enabling them to experience and discover the place of movement and dance in personal development, liturgy and religious education.
A major factor in establishing CHOROS has been the interest and talent of Barbara Cook. Barbara is an experienced dancer, choreo- grapher and instructor who has worked with a small company of dancers in offering litur- gical dance opportunities to parishes and congregations. CHOROS was established in order to promote and expand this work and to reach out to others--dancers, educators and liturgists alike--who are working in this area, often alone and unaware of the experiences and efforts of others.
The directors of CHOROS met monthly to con- tinue monitoring what is happening and to ascertain the kind of assistance it can give to those working in this area. It is current- ly on a funding program in order to be better able to realize its purpose as stated above.
By relating to a number of persons/groups interested in liturgical movement/dance, by fostering workshops and consultations, by drawing on and sharing the experience of those working in this field, it hopes to recover and discover the place and possibilities for movement in liturgy both by artists and con- gregation.

From Oregon/SW Washington, Susan Cole: Rather than gather news we decided to meet and show what we've done. That way others can learn and ask questions about lighting, logistics, response, etc. We have an active, enthusiastic and cooperative membership. Right now we are busy planning the last minute details of a two day workshop to be held May 6 and 7. Judy Rock will be conducting this workshop. An idea to share with others -- since Judy is a Presbyterian by ordination - we negotiated with the Presbytery of the Cascades which includes Oregon/SW Washington, to pay for part of the workshop expense. We also have appointed a committee to study by- laws, etc., so that we may apply for member- ship as a region according to the newly pro- posed by-laws. We would like to strongly support the regional concept and would be willing to share ideas with any other in- terested area.
