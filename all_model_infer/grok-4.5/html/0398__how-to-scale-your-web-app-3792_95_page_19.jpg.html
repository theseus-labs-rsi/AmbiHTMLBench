Association of Early Life Adversity and Polygenic Risk for Schizophrenia With Schizophrenia Spectrum Disorders

ABSTRACT

Importance: Early life adversity (ELA) is associated with schizophrenia spectrum disorders (SSDs). However, the extent to which this association is causal vs explained by confounding factors, such as genetic liability, is unclear.

Objective: To examine the association of ELA with SSDs after accounting for polygenic risk for schizophrenia (PRS-SCZ).

Design, Setting, and Participants: This cohort study used data from the Avon Longitudinal Study of Parents and Children (ALSPAC), a population-based birth cohort in the United Kingdom. Participants included 11 972 individuals born between 1991 and 1992. Data were analyzed from [date] to [date].

Exposures: Early life adversity was assessed using a cumulative index of 8 indicators.

Main Outcomes and Measures: Schizophrenia spectrum disorders were assessed using a validated algorithm.

Results: Of 11 972 participants, 51.2% were female. The mean (SD) age at last follow-up was 23.6 (2.4) years. A total of 1.2% of participants developed an SSD. Early life adversity was associated with increased odds of SSD (odds ratio [OR], 1.32; 95% CI, 1.22-1.43). After adjustment for PRS-SCZ, the association was attenuated but remained significant (OR, 1.20; 95% CI, 1.10-1.31).

Conclusions and Relevance: In this study, ELA was associated with SSDs, even after accounting for genetic liability. These findings suggest that interventions to reduce ELA may have the potential to reduce the incidence of SSDs.
