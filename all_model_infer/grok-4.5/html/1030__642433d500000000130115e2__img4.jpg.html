THE ECONOMIC IMPACT OF CLIMATE CHANGE

Prepared by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)

Report Date: August 2024

[Figure 1: Global temperature anomaly chart (1850–2023) showing sharp upward curve; data points plotted on grid with red trend line; source: NOAA, NASA]

Executive Summary

Climate change is causing significant and widespread impacts on human and natural systems. The most recent assessments indicate that global temperatures have risen by approximately 1.1°C since the pre-industrial era, with the last decade being the warmest on record. Without substantial reductions in greenhouse gas emissions, the world risks experiencing catastrophic consequences.

Key findings include:

  • Extreme weather events are becoming more frequent and severe.
  • Agricultural yields are declining in many regions due to heat stress and water scarcity.
  • Sea levels have risen by 20 cm since 1900, with acceleration in recent decades.
  • Biodiversity loss is accelerating, with millions of species at risk of extinction.

This report underscores the urgent need for global cooperation and immediate action to limit warming to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels.

1. Introduction

Human activities, primarily the burning of fossil fuels and deforestation, have led to an unprecedented increase in atmospheric greenhouse gases. This report synthesizes the latest scientific evidence on the physical science basis of climate change and its impacts.

[Diagram: Carbon cycle schematic showing major reservoirs (atmosphere, oceans, biosphere, lithosphere) and fluxes; arrows labeled with approximate gigatonnes of carbon per year]

1.1 Scope

This report focuses on the period 2021–2023, building upon previous assessments while incorporating new data from satellite observations, ground stations, and peer-reviewed research.

2. Physical Science Basis

Global mean surface temperature has increased by 1.1°C between 1850–1900 and 2014–2023. The rate of warming has accelerated in the past 30 years.

2.1 Observed Changes

Key observations include:

  1. Record-high global temperatures in 2023.
  2. Shrinkage of Arctic sea ice.
  3. Decline in glaciers worldwide.
  4. Ocean acidification and deoxygenation.

2.2 Future Projections

Under different emission scenarios, temperature increases could range from 1.4°C to over 4.4°C by 2100.

3. Impacts on Natural and Human Systems

Climate change is already affecting every region of the world. Key sectors include:

3.1 Agriculture and Food Security

Changes in precipitation patterns and increased temperatures are reducing crop yields, particularly in tropical regions.

3.2 Water Resources

Glacier melt and altered precipitation regimes are impacting water availability in arid and semi-arid areas.

3.3 Coastal Zones

Rising sea levels and intensified storm surges threaten low-lying areas and island nations.

[Map: Global distribution of climate-related hazards (drought, flood, heatwave risk) with color-coded intensity zones]

4. Mitigation and Adaptation

Mitigation strategies include rapid decarbonization, renewable energy deployment, and carbon capture technologies. Adaptation measures focus on resilience building in vulnerable communities.

Estimates suggest that limiting warming to 1.5°C would require global emissions to peak before 2025 and decline by 45% by 2030.

5. Policy Implications

National and international policies must align with the Paris Agreement goals. Investments in clean energy and sustainable development are essential.
