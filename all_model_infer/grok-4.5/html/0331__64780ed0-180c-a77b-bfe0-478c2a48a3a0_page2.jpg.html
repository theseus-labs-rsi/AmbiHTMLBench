Algebra & Geometry

Algebra

Algebra is the branch of mathematics concerning the study of symbols, variables, and the rules for manipulating them. It forms the foundation for advanced mathematics and applications in science and engineering.

A central concept in algebra is the polynomial equation. The quadratic equation is the simplest non-linear polynomial equation and serves as a key example.

Quadratic Equation & Solution $$ ax^2 + bx + c = 0 $$ $$ x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} $$

The discriminant, $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$, determines the nature of the roots. If $\Delta > 0$, two distinct real roots exist. If $\Delta = 0$, there is one repeated real root. If $\Delta < 0$, the roots are complex conjugates.

Algebraic manipulation, such as factoring, completing the square, and using the quadratic formula, provides the tools to solve these equations.

Geometry

Geometry explores the properties of space, figures, and the relationships between them. A classic problem involves the relationship between circles and triangles.

A triangle whose vertices lie on a circle is called a cyclic triangle. The circle is known as the circumcircle. For an equilateral triangle inscribed in a circle, the center of the circle (O) coincides with the centroid of the triangle.

If the side length of the equilateral triangle is $s$, the radius $R$ of the circumscribed circle is given by a simple formula:

Circumradius Formula $$ R = \frac{s}{\sqrt{3}} $$

This relationship is derived from the geometry of 30-60-90 triangles formed by the radii and the apothem.
