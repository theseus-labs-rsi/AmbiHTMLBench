GLOBAL WARMING AND CLIMATE CHANGE: A SCIENTIFIC PERSPECTIVE

Prepared by: Climate Science Research Institute

Abstract

The Earth’s climate has been undergoing significant changes over the past century, primarily driven by human activities. This document provides a comprehensive overview of the scientific evidence supporting global warming and climate change. It includes sections on causes, impacts, and proposed solutions.

Global warming refers to the long-term increase in the average temperature of the Earth’s climate system. Since the pre-industrial era, the average global surface temperature has risen by approximately 1.1°C. This warming is unequivocally caused by the accumulation of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere, mainly carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), and nitrous oxide (N2O), which trap heat radiating from the planet.

Causes of Global Warming

1. Greenhouse Gas Emissions

Human activities, particularly the burning of fossil fuels (coal, oil, and natural gas), deforestation, and industrial processes, have led to a sharp increase in greenhouse gases. According to the IPCC (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change), the concentration of CO2 in the atmosphere has increased from 280 parts per million (ppm) in 1750 to over 420 ppm today.

2. Deforestation

Trees act as carbon sinks, absorbing CO2 through photosynthesis. However, due to agricultural expansion, urbanization, and logging, large areas of forests have been cleared, reducing the planet’s capacity to mitigate climate change.

3. Industrial Activities

Manufacturing, transportation, and energy production contribute significantly to emissions. The transportation sector alone accounts for about 14% of global CO2 emissions.

Impacts of Climate Change

1. Rising Sea Levels

Due to thermal expansion of ocean water and melting ice caps, global sea levels have risen by about 20 cm since 1900. Projections indicate a potential rise of 0.28 to 0.55 meters by 2100 under current emission scenarios.

2. Extreme Weather Events

Heatwaves, droughts, floods, and hurricanes have become more frequent and intense. The number of record-breaking hot days has increased by 50% since 1970.

3. Ecosystems and Biodiversity

Coral reefs are bleaching at an unprecedented rate. Approximately 1 million species face extinction due to habitat loss and changing climate conditions.

4. Human Health

Changes in climate patterns contribute to the spread of diseases like malaria, dengue fever, and heat-related illnesses. Vulnerable populations, including children and the elderly, are disproportionately affected.

Proposed Solutions

1. Renewable Energy Adoption

Transitioning to solar, wind, and hydroelectric power can significantly reduce carbon emissions. Many countries have set ambitious targets for renewable energy integration.

2. Afforestation and Reforestation

Planting new trees and restoring existing forests can sequester carbon and restore ecosystems.

3. Policy and International Cooperation

Agreements like the Paris Agreement aim to limit global temperature rise to well below 2°C. National governments are implementing carbon pricing, emission regulations, and clean technology incentives.

4. Energy Efficiency Improvements

Enhancing efficiency in buildings, vehicles, and industrial processes reduces the demand for fossil fuels.

Conclusion

Global warming and climate change are real and urgent threats to humanity and the planet. Scientific consensus is clear, and collective action is essential to mitigate these impacts. Individuals, businesses, and governments must work together to achieve a sustainable future.
