THE THEORY OF THE QUANTUM HALL EFFECT

The quantum Hall effect is one of the most important phenomena in condensed-matter physics, first discovered in 1980 by Klaus von Klitzing. It provides a direct way to realize a quantum of electrical resistance with unprecedented accuracy and is used to define the ohm in the SI system of units. This paper presents a comprehensive review of the theoretical foundations of the effect, including the role of Landau levels, gauge invariance, and edge states.

1. Introduction

The discovery of the integer quantum Hall effect in a two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) under a strong perpendicular magnetic field led to a Nobel Prize in Physics in 1985. The effect manifests as plateaus in the Hall resistivity ρ_xy at values h/(n e²), where n is an integer, and vanishing longitudinal resistivity ρ_xx.

This paper focuses on the theoretical derivation from first principles, using the concepts of Berry phase and topological invariants.

2. The Hamiltonian of a 2DEG in a Magnetic Field

The single-particle Hamiltonian in the presence of a magnetic field B = (0,0,B) is given by

$$ H = \frac{1}{2m} \left( \mathbf{p} + \frac{e}{c} \mathbf{A} \right)^2 $$

where A is the vector potential. In the Landau gauge A = (0, Bx, 0), the eigenstates are the famous Landau levels.

For a 2DEG with parabolic dispersion, the energy spectrum is

$$ E_n = \hbar \omega_c \left( n + \frac{1}{2} \right) $$

with ω_c = eB / mc the cyclotron frequency.

3. Degeneracy of Landau Levels

Each Landau level has a degeneracy per unit area given by

$$ N_\phi / A = \frac{eB}{h c} = \frac{B}{\phi_0} $$

where φ₀ = hc/e is the magnetic flux quantum.

4. The Integer Quantum Hall Effect

In the presence of disorder (scattering centers), the Fermi level lies in localized states between Landau levels. As the magnetic field is swept, the Hall conductivity σ_xy shows quantized plateaus at integer multiples of e²/h.

The longitudinal conductivity vanishes within the plateau regions because the extended states are at the center of each Landau level.

6. Edge States and the Chiral Luttinger Liquid

On the sample edges, the magnetic field confines the electrons into chiral modes that propagate in one direction. The edge current is carried by these gapless excitations, explaining the dissipationless transport.

The edge theory is a Luttinger liquid with central charge c = 1 for the integer case.

7. Topological Invariance and the TKNN Invariant

The Hall conductivity is quantized because it is a topological invariant (Thouless–Kohmoto–Nightingale–den Nijs or TKNN number):

$$ \sigma_{xy} = \frac{e^2}{h} C $$

where C is the Chern number of the filled bands.

For a system with periodic boundary conditions, the Berry curvature integral over the Brillouin zone yields the integer Chern number.

8. Experimental Realization and Applications

High-mobility GaAs/AlGaAs heterostructures are the standard platform. The effect is observed at millikelvin temperatures and fields of several Tesla. Applications include precision resistance standards and quantum metrology.

9. Conclusions

The quantum Hall effect demonstrates the power of topology in quantum mechanics. It has not only revolutionized our understanding of 2D electron systems but also provides the most accurate realization of the resistance quantum.
